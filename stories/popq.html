PopQ

Private — played at home

My partner and I are expecting a girl, and we are excited. We wanted to learn first aid for babies before she arrives.

The courses for expecting parents in the Netherlands cost quite some money. They also only cover the Netherlands, and we will be spending time in Sweden as well as Amsterdam. So I built the course myself, for the two of us.

It follows the Dutch syllabus for first aid on children and the current Dutch resuscitation guidelines, in twelve modules. Where Sweden does something differently, the slide says so: which number to call, which app sends a volunteer to a cardiac arrest. Every slide lists its sources, so we could check anything that surprised us.

Then the questions took over

Each module ends with multiple choice questions. When we got to that part, I thought it would be fun to make a trivia game out of it. We have hosted a few trivia games before, as part of bigger events with friends, and this was most of one already.

So the course moved to the TV. A host starts a game, everyone scans a code and answers on their own phone, and the TV shows the right answer, who picked what, and the scores. It turned out to be quite fun.

What it is now

PopQ plays two kinds of quiz. A course teaches first and asks afterwards, like the first aid one. A trivia is only for fun: it opens on a question and keeps going.

The hosts

Every quiz has its own pair of hosts. Each one has a name, a face, a voice and a character, chosen to fit the topic. While a host is talking, their face shows in a corner of the TV.

  • The first aid course is taught by Noor, a calm teddy-bear nurse who never makes light of an emergency. Beside her is Pip, a slightly worried duckling who asks what a new parent would ask.
  • The Thanksgiving trivia has Mercy, a formal Pilgrim mouse from 1620 who is amazed by everything modern, and Dex, a shopping robot from Black Friday who sounds like a sales announcer.
  • The trivia about recorded sound has Mack, a vintage studio microphone with a late-night radio voice, and Bit, a cassette tape who is the studio's eager intern.

They do not read the slides out. They welcome the room, open each round, and ask every question in their own words, so someone who is only listening can still answer. Sometimes the two of them talk to each other.

They also react to the room. One line is for when everyone answers before the host has finished asking, one for when everyone gets it right, and one for when nobody does.

The voices are generated, and the character description steers how each line is read. All of it is recorded ahead of time, so nothing is generated while a game is being played.
