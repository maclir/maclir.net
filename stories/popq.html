PopQ

Private — played at home

We are expecting a girl. Before she arrives, we wanted to know what to do when something goes wrong.

There are courses for exactly this. In the Netherlands they are aimed at expecting parents, and they cost quite some money. They also teach the Dutch answer: which number to call, which app to open. We will be spending time in Sweden as well as Amsterdam, and the answer there is not the same.

So I built the course instead, for my partner and me.

The course

It follows the Dutch syllabus for first aid on children and the current resuscitation guidelines, in twelve modules. Each one explains first and asks afterwards. Where Sweden does it differently, the slide says so.

Every slide lists its sources, on purpose. This is not a subject where anyone should take my word for it.

Then the questions

Each module ends in multiple choice questions. Somewhere in that part it stopped feeling like a course. We have hosted a few trivia games over the years, as part of bigger events with friends, and this was most of one already.

So it moved to the TV, and it turned out to be quite fun.

How it works

A host starts a game on the TV. Everyone scans the code and answers on their own phone. When the last answer is in, the TV shows the right one, who picked what, and the scores. Players need no app and no account, just a name.

It plays two kinds of quiz now. A course teaches and then tests, like the first aid one. A trivia skips the teaching and opens on a question.

The hosts

A quiz read off a screen in silence is homework. So every quiz has a pair of hosts, each with a name, a face, a voice and a character that fits the topic. Whoever is talking shows in a corner of the TV.

They do not read the slides out. They welcome the room, open each round and ask every question in their own words, so you can answer without looking up. Sometimes they talk to each other instead of to you.

They also react. Answer before the host has finished asking and you get a line about that. Everyone right gets one, and so does nobody right.

The voices are generated, and the character is what steers how a line gets read. All of it is recorded ahead of time, so nothing is being generated while people are playing.
