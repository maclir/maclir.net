PopQ

Private — played at home

My partner and I are expecting a girl, and we are excited. We wanted to learn first aid for babies before she arrives.

The courses for expecting parents in the Netherlands cost quite some money. They also only cover the Netherlands, and we will be spending time in Sweden as well as Amsterdam. So I built the course myself, for the two of us.

It follows the Dutch syllabus for first aid on children and the current Dutch resuscitation guidelines, in twelve modules. Where Sweden does something differently, the slide says so: which number to call, which app sends a volunteer to a cardiac arrest. Every slide lists its sources, so we could check anything that surprised us.

Then the questions took over

Each module ends with multiple choice questions. When we got to that part, I thought it would be fun to make a trivia game out of it. We have hosted a few trivia games before, as part of bigger events with friends, and this was most of one already.

So the course moved to the TV. A host starts a game, everyone scans a code and answers on their own phone, and the TV shows the right answer, who picked what, and the scores. It turned out to be quite fun.

What it is now

PopQ plays two kinds of quiz. A course teaches first and asks afterwards, like the first aid one. A trivia is only for fun: it opens on a question and keeps going. There are two of those so far, one about Thanksgiving and one about how sound gets from a microphone to your headphones.

Each quiz also has its own pair of hosts, who read the questions aloud and react to the room.
